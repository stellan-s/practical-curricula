# Effective Learning & Spaced Repetition

> This curriculum was developed with AI assistance and has not been independently validated.

**Goal:** Build a repeatable system for understanding, retaining, and applying material from any of your existing curricula.

**Format:** Seven modules, approximately **six hours**, followed by a 14-day trial.

> Use one real subject throughout. Do not study “learning” without applying it—pick one of your existing curricula.

## 1. How learning actually works — 50 min

**Watch**

- Stephen Chew: [Beliefs That Make You Fail—or Succeed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RH95h36NChI)
- Stephen Chew: [What Students Should Know About How People Learn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9O7y7XEC66M)
- Stephen Chew: [Cognitive Principles for Optimizing Learning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1xeHh5DnCIw)

**Exercise:** Audit your present method: list what you currently reread, highlight, practise, retrieve, and merely recognise. Circle anything that creates confidence without testing knowledge.

## 2. Understand before memorising — 60 min

**Watch**

- Stephen Chew: [Putting Principles for Learning into Practice](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E9GrOxhYZdQ)
- The Learning Scientists: [Elaboration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gQRzW24KrDc)
- The Learning Scientists: [Concrete Examples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7wF0lCBMh9Y)

**Exercise:** For one lesson, produce:

- a five-node concept map;
- three “why/how” questions;
- two concrete examples;
- one connection to something you already know.

## 3. Retrieval practice — 45 min

**Watch**

- The Learning Scientists: [Retrieval Practice](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pjrqc6UMDKM)
- Benjamin Keep: [How to Do Free Recall](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FatnXnlwAc4)

**Exercise:** Close everything and write a five-minute brain dump about yesterday’s material. Check it against the source in another colour, then answer the missed questions again from memory.

## 4. Spacing and forgetting — 45 min

**Watch**

- The Learning Scientists: [Spaced Practice](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3WJYp98eys8)
- Benjamin Keep: [The Problem with Active Recall and Spaced Repetition](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=--Hu2w0s72Y)

**Exercise:** Schedule retrieval of the same material after approximately **1, 3, 7, and 14 days**. Expand intervals when recall is easy; shorten them after failure.

## 5. Formulating good questions — 75 min

**Watch**

- The AnKing: [Making Effective Anki Cards](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b1NIQeFYQFQ)

**Read selectively**

- Piotr Wozniak: [Twenty Rules of Formulating Knowledge](https://www.supermemo.com/en/blog/twenty-rules-of-formulating-knowledge) — focus on rules 1–5 and 8–9.

**Exercise:** Turn one lesson into ten prompts. Each must:

- test one idea;
- have an unambiguous answer;
- require recall, not recognition;
- contain enough context;
- test knowledge you already understand.

Delete any card that merely reproduces a paragraph.

## 6. Implement the system in Anki — 60 min

**Resources**

- [Anki: Getting Started](https://docs.ankiweb.net/getting-started.html)
- The AnKing: [2025 Anki Settings and FSRS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uo-qQvOZDfg)
- Current reference: [Anki FSRS documentation](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html#fsrs)

**Exercise:** Create one deck, enable FSRS, leave desired retention around **90%**, add your ten prompts, and complete the first review. Ignore add-ons and visual customisation.

## 7. Transfer, discrimination, and real skill — 60 min

**Watch**

- The Learning Scientists: [Interleaving](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kV64Bu6sec0)
- Benjamin Keep: [What Study Gurus Get Wrong About Learning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iW7Fp9Mtq1Y)

**Exercise:** Create one mixed session containing:

1. factual recall;
2. explanation in your own words;
3. comparison between two similar concepts;
4. one unfamiliar application problem;
5. feedback and correction.

## 14-day capstone

For two weeks:

- Learn from your chosen curriculum three times weekly.
- Retrieve before reopening notes.
- Review due prompts daily for 10–15 minutes.
- Once weekly, solve or produce something without guidance.
- At day 14, test yourself with new questions—not your existing cards.

**Success criterion:** You can explain and apply the material after a week without relying on recognition.

Spaced repetition preserves accessible knowledge; it does **not** replace projects, problem-solving, musical practice, or other real performance.
